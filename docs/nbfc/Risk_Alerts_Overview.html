Risk Alerts: what we show, and where every number comes from

Our lending partners see risk the way an instrument panel shows an engine — a small set of cards and alerts, each one the answer to a question we can defend with data. This document lists every card and alert we can put on screen, what is printed inside each one, which database column it was read from, and who is allowed to change the thresholds.

01

The idea in one page

A risk alert is a testable statement about borrowers, answered with real data — never a vibe and never a guess.

Take one: “Borrowers who are past due on their EMI and whose vehicle has stopped reporting GPS are hiding the asset.” To answer it, the platform pulls the loan book from our CRM database, pulls the vehicle's live telemetry from the separate IoT database, joins the two on the vehicle registration number, runs the test, and writes down the verdict: a severity colour, a count, and the actual rows that triggered it. The NBFC then sees one card.

Everything in this document hangs off that one picture. Two things are worth carrying into the room with you:

  • The colour measures prevalence, not money. “High Alert” means a large share of the assessed population tripped the test — it is not a rupee figure.
  • A test that could not run says so. It is shown as Inconclusive, never as a green OK. A coverage gap is not a clean bill of health, and the product refuses to present it as one.

04

The risk-card catalogue

There are five governed cards — written by our engineers, reviewed, and deterministic. Alongside them the platform keeps up to twelve agent-authored cards active at a time: an AI agent proposes a new hypothesis, writes the Python that tests it, and that Python is executed against real data in a sandbox. Agent cards carry a small AI badge and, until a human promotes them, they are capped at Warning — they may raise a concern, they may not declare a High Alert.

The five governed cards

Bold thresholds in the “trips when” column are read live from the governed rule table — a Risk Head can change them (§12). The 5 pp and 20 km/day figures are documented constants, because the rule catalogue has no entry for them.

The point worth making out loud

Every card counts only the population it can honestly measure. A vehicle with no telemetry is excluded from the denominator and reported as a coverage note — it is never counted as a healthy 0 km. That distinction is why an ingestion outage no longer fires a false High Alert across the whole fleet.

Card by card, in full

7-day usage cliff

usage-drop-7d
Hypothesis
Borrowers whose 7-day km is at least 40% below their prior 7-day baseline. Sharp utilisation drops historically precede payment delinquency by 2–3 weeks.
Reads
Loan book (nbfc_loans) joined to 14 days of daily mileage from the IoT distance_rollup table.
Method
Sum km for the last 7 days and the 7 days before that, per vehicle. Vehicles with a prior week under 50 km are excluded as having no usable baseline.
Evidence
Top 10 vehicles by drop %, each with prior km, recent km and drop %; a bar chart; and a note stating how many loans had a usable baseline.

Past-due + telemetry silent

dpd-7-no-telemetry
Hypothesis
Loans past due whose vehicles have stopped reporting GPS. The combination signals concealment risk, not just a flat battery.
Reads
nbfc_loans.current_dpd for the overdue pool, then the IoT vehicle_state table for seconds since the last GPS fix.
Method
Two governed thresholds combine: days past due (default 30) and GPS silence (default 24 h). A loan with no telemetry row at all also counts as silent.
Evidence
Up to 10 loans with their DPD, EMI, vehicle number and seconds since last GPS.

Vehicle far from its usual operating area

geo-shift
Hypothesis
Vehicles operating far outside their usual area. Possible asset diversion.
Reads
30 days of GPS fixes per vehicle from the IoT database.
Method
The vehicle's “home” is the most-visited ~11 km GPS cell over 30 days, and that cell needs at least 50 fixes to count as a home at all. Distance from home is compared against the governed 100 km rule. Vehicles without an establishable home are excluded and reported as a coverage gap.
Evidence
Vehicle number, distance from home in km, current lat/lon and home lat/lon.

Accelerated battery degradation

battery-soh-decay
Hypothesis
Vehicles whose State-of-Health has dropped more than 5 percentage points in 30 days. Affects both asset value and operator income.
Reads
Oldest and newest SOH reading in a 30-day window, from IoT CAN telemetry.
Method
Signed delta between the two ends. Only vehicles with a reading at both ends are measurable; the rest are excluded, and if none qualify the card returns Inconclusive.
Evidence
The 10 worst decayers with their start SOH, end SOH and delta, plus a note that the 5 pp threshold is a documented constant, not a tunable rule.

Active loan, low utilisation

low-utilization-active-loan
Hypothesis
Loans with a current EMI obligation whose vehicles averaged under 20 km/day over 14 days. The operator may be churning or sub-letting.
Reads
Active-EMI loans, joined to 14 days of daily mileage.
Method
Average over the days the vehicle actually reported — never over a fixed 14. A missing day is a pipeline gap, not a zero-km day. Vehicles reporting on fewer than 7 days are excluded as unmeasurable.
Evidence
Vehicle number, average km/day, days reported and EMI amount — plus explicit counts of how many vehicles had no telemetry and how many had too thin a window.

Why the evidence table matters commercially

A lender cannot act on a number it cannot audit. Every card carries the rows that produced it and the thresholds it was judged by, snapshotted at run time — so a card read six months from now is still read against the rules that were in force when it was written, not today's.

06

Severity, and how much to trust it

Two separate axes. Severity answers “how widespread is this?” Verdict source answers “who actually did the arithmetic?” A card is only trustworthy when both are good.

Five severities, four tabs

StateMeansTab
High AlertTest ran; the affected share crossed the card's high cutoffHigh Alert
WarningTest ran; the share crossed the warn cutoff — or an unvetted AI card was capped down from HighWarning
OKTest ran and found nothing. Only ever means this.OK
InconclusiveTest could not run — no measurable population, or the sandbox was unavailableInconclusive
ErrorTest crashed, or the IoT database was unreachableInconclusive

Inconclusive and Error share a tab — to an operator both mean “no verdict” — but they stay distinct in the database so we can tell a crash from a coverage gap.

Four verdict sources

SourceLabel on screenMeaning
hand_codedComputedOne of our five reviewed TypeScript evaluators did the arithmetic
sandboxComputed (sandbox)Agent-authored Python, executed deterministically on real data
noneNot computedNo verdict was produced. The card shows no counts at all.
legacy_llmUnverified (legacy)A pre-upgrade row where a language model may have stated the numbers itself. Flagged in violet, banner at the top of the page, and replaced on the next run.
The governing rule

A language model may propose a hypothesis and write the code that tests it. It may never state the verdict. Severity and counts are computed from data or they do not exist — and an unvetted agent hypothesis cannot raise a High Alert until a human has reviewed it and pressed Promote.

These are per-battery, not per-portfolio. Six of them are evaluated the instant a telemetry packet arrives; two fire on the absence of a packet and are swept every 15 minutes. At most one open alert exists per battery per rule, so a stuck fault does not spam the feed.

What a feed row shows

In the “Needs attention now” feed each alert renders as one line: a severity dot, the rule name, a detail clause built from the alert's captured context, then a metadata strip of battery serial · borrower name · city · relative time, and a View case link through to the borrower's loan. Each alert also stores a payload snapshot of the exact inputs that fired it — for example the SOC reading, the charger state and the threshold — so the firing context is reproducible without going back to raw telemetry.

08

Borrower scores: CDS and PCI

Two scores are computed per active loan every night. They are the repayment-behaviour half of the risk picture — the half that owes nothing to telemetry.

CDS — Credit Default Score

Runs at midnight. Looks back over the last 6 EMI instalments and asks how badly, and how recently, the borrower has slipped. Each instalment gets a weight — 0 for paid, 0.5 for up to a week late, 0.7 for later than that, 0.85–1.0 for an active overdue depending on days, 1.0 for a miss — multiplied by a recency factor that fades from 1.0 for the newest to a floor of 0.4. Consecutive misses add a streak penalty. Stale telemetry adds a further risk term. The result is normalised to 0–100.

The score is banded for display — Low 0–39 · Medium 40–69 · High 70–84 · Very High 85+ — using the three governed CDS boundaries.

Every run also records a confidence: High with six or more EMI records and telemetry ingested within 12 hours, Medium with three to five records or stale telemetry, Low below three records or when a restructuring flag is set. Each run inserts a new score row rather than overwriting — an immutable history, per RBI's Digital Lending Directions.

PCI — Payment Consistency Index

Runs fifteen minutes later. A cleaner, single-purpose measure of habit: PCI = Σ(EMI score × weight) ÷ Σ(weights), where each instalment scores 1.0 if paid on time, 0.5 if paid within a week late, 0.0 if missed — and the newest instalment carries the heaviest weight, stepping down linearly to the oldest. The result lands between 0 and 1 and is banded below 0.40 high concern · 0.40–0.75 monitoring · above 0.75 healthy, with the concern boundary read from the governed pci_concern rule.

Because the weights are explicit, the platform can show why a score is what it is: every instalment's score, its weight and its exact contribution to the final number.

Which of these actually becomes an alert row

Only PCI does, today. When a loan's PCI falls below the concern threshold, the nightly job writes a borrower risk alert of type pci_low — marked critical if the score is below half the threshold, otherwise high — carrying the score, the threshold and the EMI window that produced it. That row is what appears in the “Needs attention now” feed. CDS is displayed as a score and a band on the borrower, and it feeds the risk cards' context, but it does not itself raise a feed row.

09

Portfolio anomaly flags

The only family aimed at us rather than at the lender. Each night the platform rolls up every NBFC's whole book and tests three thresholds:

MetricBreaches when
Delinquency rateabove 15%
Recovery ratebelow 70%
Average DPDabove 30 days

Two or three breaches raise a red flag, exactly one raises amber, none clears any open flag. At most one open flag exists per NBFC at a time; if the reasons drift, the existing flag is updated in place rather than duplicated, and when metrics recover the flag is closed with a timestamp. The flag stores the list of human-readable reasons that produced it.

A deliberate design choice

The portfolio alert feed reads telemetry_alerts from our own CRM database, not from the IoT server. That means the command centre renders in full even when the IoT link is down. The risk cards do reach across, and when they cannot, the page says so in an amber banner and marks those cards Error — never OK.

The end-to-end pipeline

Four families, four independent paths, one shared discipline: raw signal → an engine that applies a rule → a durable row → a surface a human reads → an action with an audit trail.

The schedule, in one list

ScheduleJobProduces
every 15 minOffline-battery scanBattery Offline / Battery Offline Extended alerts
on each packetPer-packet rule evaluatorThe six live battery rules
00:00CDS computationA new credit-default score per active loan
00:10Anomaly flag evaluationRed/amber flags per NBFC
00:15PCI computationPayment-consistency score and band
01:30Risk-hypothesis analysisA fresh card run for every active NBFC

Schedules are the configured cron expressions; the risk analysis is fired by a crontab entry on our own VPS rather than by the hosting platform. Tenants are swept one at a time — each run calls the model and the sandbox, and a simultaneous fan-out would stampede both.

Three run-control guarantees

  • One run per lender at a time. The lock is a database constraint, not application logic — a second caller is rejected outright with a “run already in flight” response rather than being allowed to double-charge the model. A run that dies is reaped after 15 minutes.
  • Staleness is visible. A result older than 36 hours earns a Stale badge on the page header. A lender who has never had a run sees an explicit “Never run — these cards are computed live on page load” notice, not a silent blank.
  • Broken tests retire themselves. A hypothesis that errors on three consecutive runs is soft-retired with a reason. Its history is kept; it stops being run and stops being shown.

What the sandbox is allowed to do

Agent-written Python never touches our systems. It runs in a separate token-authenticated service with no network and no database access, restricted to a vetted list of language constructs and to pandas and numpy only, with a 30-second execution ceiling. It receives three prepared tables — loans, vehicle states, daily kilometres — and returns a verdict object. If the sandbox is unavailable, every agent card that run is Inconclusive. There is no fallback that lets a model answer in its place.

Who controls the thresholds

Eight numbers decide when most alerts fire. They live in a governed table, are edited through an admin screen, and every change passes a two-person approval gate. Crucially, the evaluators read them at run time and snapshot them into the card's evidence — so changing a rule changes tomorrow's cards without rewriting yesterday's.

Three further controls

  • Card visibility allowlist. Per NBFC, an iTarang admin ticks which cards that partner sees. It is a strict allowlist — a card with no entry is hidden, and the filter runs at display time so no re-run can slip past it.
  • Promote. An agent-authored hypothesis is capped at Warning until a human reviews the question and the code and promotes it. Only then can it declare a High Alert.
  • Retire. A hypothesis that turns out to be badly posed is retired: its history is kept, but it is no longer run and no longer rendered.

From alert to action

An alert that cannot be acted on is decoration. The Risk page publishes the action framework alongside the cards, so the approval cost of every response is visible before anyone reaches for it.

ActionApproval requiredReversibleAudit log
Send Payment ReminderSingle — NBFC Usern/aYes — automatic
Request Field VisitSingle — NBFC ManagerYesYes — reason required
Request ImmobilisationDual — Risk Head + OpsYes — re-mobilised on EMI settlementYes — borrower notice preview required
Review for Loan RestructuringDual — Risk Head + Credit ManagerDepends on termsYes — before/after terms logged
Flag for RecoverySingle — Risk HeadNo — permanentYes

The tiering is the argument: the higher the impact on the borrower, the more people must sign, and the more the system insists on a written reason. Immobilisation — remotely disabling a financed battery — sits at the top and cannot be executed by one person.

Immobilisation — borrower notice preview

Directly beneath the action table the page publishes the five things any immobilisation notice must carry, per the RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025:

  1. Lender identity
  2. Loan Service Provider identity
  3. Outstanding amount and the steps to restore service
  4. Grievance channel
  5. Plain, non-coercive language

The lender's own legal name, grievance URL and helpline are filled in from their profile, so the preview is the actual notice, not a template.

Glossary

TermMeaning
DPDDays Past Due — how many days late the borrower's EMI is.
EMIEquated Monthly Instalment — the fixed monthly loan payment.
CDSCredit Default Score, 0–100. Weighted recent repayment behaviour plus a consecutive-miss penalty and a stale-telemetry term.
PCIPayment Consistency Index, 0–1. Recency-weighted share of instalments paid on time.
SOCState of Charge — how full the battery is right now, as a percentage.
SOHState of Health — how much capacity the battery has permanently lost. 100% is new; below roughly 60% is end of life.
BMSBattery Management System — the on-pack controller that reports faults.
Telemetry silentThe vehicle has not sent a GPS fix within the governed window. Either the device failed, or someone disconnected it.
HypothesisA testable statement about the portfolio. One hypothesis, tested for one lender at one moment, is one card.
EvidenceThe actual rows that triggered a finding, plus the methodology notes and the thresholds used — stored with the verdict.
ImmobilisationRemotely disabling a financed battery so the vehicle cannot be driven. Dual approval, borrower notice, fully audited.
TenantOne NBFC on the platform. Every number on every screen is scoped to a single tenant.
15

Known gaps — read before you demo

Internal only. None of these break the story; all of them are things a sharp question could expose, so it is better to own them.

GapWhat it means in a demo
One name, two systems The sidebar item reads “Risk Alerts” and opens the hypothesis-card page — but the “open alerts” counter on that same nav tile is fed by the borrower alert table, a different system. The number and the page do not describe the same thing.
Two battery-alert stores Battery alerts exist both in our CRM database (raised by our own rule evaluators) and in a separate table on the IoT server. The portfolio feed reads the first; parts of the battery page read the second. They can show different counts.
CDS raises no alert row An alert type for a high credit-default score is defined but never written at runtime. Only the PCI concern alert is produced. A very high CDS shows on the borrower but does not reach the feed.
Battery page score bands are hard-coded The battery monitoring view looks up its CDS band boundaries under key names that do not exist in the rule catalogue, so it always falls back to 40 / 70 and never applies the governed “Very High” cut at 85. Changing the rule does not move those bands.
The offline flag is written, not read An extended battery outage sets a flag intended to feed the credit score. The scoring job does not currently read it back.
Migration drift across environments The important one. Several of the upgrades this document describes — verdict provenance, the nightly cron, the per-partner card allowlist — are recorded as applied on some environments and not others. Confirm which database the demo points at before claiming any of them. If the five governed hypotheses were never seeded on that database, the page renders with no computed cards at all.
Before any live demo

Check three things: the IoT link is up (otherwise every telemetry card shows Error), the tenant has a card allowlist configured (otherwise the page is empty by design), and a run has happened in the last 36 hours (otherwise the header shows Stale).
